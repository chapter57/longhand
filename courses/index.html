Who it's for

For anyone who skipped the reading, skimmed the notes, or never got the chance.

Maybe you studied business, or nursing, or engineering, or went straight to work. Maybe you were assigned The Odyssey at sixteen and weren't ready for it. Great Reads is for adults who want to finally read the books everyone refers to, and understand why they're still talked about. No background needed, no exams, and no one to impress.

How it works

  • Read a little every day. About three hours a week, split into daily portions. Each season runs twelve weeks.
  • Get the story behind the book. A letter every Monday sets up the week's reading: who wrote it, what was happening, what to watch for, and why it lasted.
  • Write by hand. Each week comes with journal prompts, meant for pen and paper. The point is your own thinking, in your own handwriting. No AI.
  • Finish something. Each season ends with a short essay about one of the books. The best are published in Longhand Reviews, under your name.

Take one season or all four. They follow history in order, but each one stands on its own.

The reading listtwenty-four works, four seasons

Winter · January to March

Beginnings: The Ancient World

Where Western stories, questions and ideas start: a man trying to get home, a king who can't escape the truth, a philosopher on trial for asking questions.

  • Homer, The Odyssey
  • Sappho, poems and fragments
  • Sophocles, Oedipus the King and Antigone
  • Plato, the trial and death of Socrates (Apology, Crito, Phaedo)
  • The Book of Job
  • Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

Spring · April to June

The Self Appears: Dante to Shakespeare

How writers learned to look inward: a journey through hell, a crowd of pilgrims telling tales, a woman's visions, the first essays, and the most famous play ever written.

  • Dante, Inferno
  • Chaucer, The Canterbury Tales (selections)
  • Julian of Norwich, Revelations of Divine Love
  • Montaigne, Essays (selections)
  • Shakespeare, Hamlet

Summer · July to September

The Age of the Novel

The nineteenth century, when the novel became the way a society talked to itself: about science, money, marriage, slavery, class and death.

  • Mary Shelley, Frankenstein
  • Jane Austen, Pride and Prejudice
  • Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass
  • Charles Dickens, Great Expectations
  • Walt Whitman and Emily Dickinson, selected poems
  • Leo Tolstoy, The Death of Ivan Ilyich

Fall · October to December

The Modern Mind

The world breaks open: a man underground, a salesman turned insect, a London day in a single book, the American dream, and an empire seen from the other side.

  • Fyodor Dostoevsky, Notes from Underground
  • Anton Chekhov, selected stories
  • Franz Kafka, The Metamorphosis
  • Virginia Woolf, Mrs. Dalloway
  • F. Scott Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby
  • Chinua Achebe, Things Fall Apart

All but one of these books are in the public domain, so you can read them free. We'll link a free edition of each, and recommend a good translation or paperback if you'd like one on your shelf.
