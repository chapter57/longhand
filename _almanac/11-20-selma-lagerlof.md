Selma Lagerlöf was born at Mårbacka, a farm in the province of Värmland, in western Sweden. As a small child she lost the use of her legs for a time, and though she learned to walk again, she was lame for the rest of her life. Kept indoors while other children played, she listened to her grandmother and her aunt tell the old stories and legends of the district, full of proud squires, trolls, ghosts and wild parties at the manor houses. She remembered all of them.

The family's money ran out, and in 1888 Mårbacka was sold. Lagerlöf, by then a schoolteacher in the town of Landskrona, in the far south of Sweden, was writing a novel out of those Värmland stories. In 1890 she entered its first chapters in a magazine competition and won. The book, *Gösta Berling's Saga* (1891), is about a defrocked priest who falls in with a band of charming, reckless cavaliers on a great Värmland estate. It became a classic, and it allowed her, in time, to give up teaching and write full-time.

Then Sweden's teachers asked her to write a geography book for schoolchildren. Lagerlöf turned it into a story. In *The Wonderful Adventures of Nils* (1906–07), a lazy, unkind farm boy is shrunk to the size of a thumb by an elf he has mistreated, and he climbs on the back of a farm goose that flies off to join a flock of wild geese, led by the wise old Akka. Flying across the whole of Sweden, Nils learns its landscapes, its history and kindness. Generations of Swedish children learned their country from it.

In 1909 Lagerlöf became the first woman to win the Nobel Prize in Literature. She used the money to buy back Mårbacka, where she lived and farmed for the rest of her life. In 1914 she became the first woman elected to the Swedish Academy. When the Soviet Union invaded Finland in the winter of 1939, she sent her Nobel gold medal to the Finnish government to help pay for its defense; touched, the Finns raised the money another way and sent it back. And in her last months, she helped the German Jewish poet Nelly Sachs and her mother escape the Nazis to Sweden. Lagerlöf died in March 1940; Sachs arrived that May, and in 1966 she, too, won the Nobel Prize.
