Adeline Virginia Stephen was born in London into a large, bookish household. Her father, Leslie Stephen, was a critic and the first editor of the *Dictionary of National Biography*, and she educated herself in his library while her brothers went to Cambridge. Her mother died when she was thirteen, and the loss brought on the first of the breakdowns that recurred throughout her life.

After her father's death, she and her siblings moved to Bloomsbury, and their house became the meeting place of the writers, painters and thinkers later called the Bloomsbury Group. In 1912 she married the writer Leonard Woolf, and in 1917 they bought a small hand press and set it up in their dining room. The Hogarth Press published her own books, T. S. Eliot's *The Waste Land*, and the first English translations of Freud.

In her novels she tried to capture the stream of thought from moment to moment. *Mrs. Dalloway* (1925) follows a London hostess through a single June day as she prepares for a party, while a shell-shocked veteran moves toward tragedy across the city. *To the Lighthouse* (1927) is about a family's summer house over ten years and a trip finally taken. *Orlando* (1928) is a playful biography of a poet who lives for three hundred years and changes sex along the way.

She was also one of the great essayists in English. *A Room of One's Own* (1929), drawn from two talks at the women's colleges of Cambridge, asked why there had been so few women writers, and answered that a woman needs money and a room of her own if she is to write fiction. In March 1941, fearing another breakdown, she drowned herself in the River Ouse, near her home in Sussex.
