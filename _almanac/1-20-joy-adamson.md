Joy Adamson was born Friederike Victoria Gessner in Troppau, in what is now the Czech Republic, and grew up in Vienna. She studied music, sculpture and medicine without settling on any of them, and in 1937, newly married, she sailed to Kenya. She stayed for the rest of her life.

In Kenya she became a painter and botanist. Over many years she made hundreds of paintings of the country's plants and of the people of its different ethnic groups, a remarkable record now held by the National Museums of Kenya. Her third husband, George Adamson, was a game warden.

In 1956, George shot a lioness that charged him and found she had left three cubs. Two were sent to a zoo in Rotterdam, but the Adamsons kept the smallest, whom they named Elsa, and raised her in their home. When Elsa was grown, they did something no one thought possible: they taught her to hunt and to survive, and released her into the wild. She came back, from time to time, to visit them, and later with cubs of her own.

Joy's book about Elsa, *Born Free* (1960), illustrated with her photographs, spent months on bestseller lists around the world and became a much-loved film. It changed how many people thought about wild animals: not as trophies or zoo exhibits, but as lives worth protecting in their own places. Adamson used the money to found a wildlife charity. She was murdered at her camp in Kenya in 1980.
