Sidonie-Gabrielle Colette was born in Saint-Sauveur-en-Puisaye, a village in Burgundy, and grew up in a house and garden she wrote about all her life, with a mother, Sido, who loved plants, animals and freedom. At twenty she married Henry Gauthier-Villars, known as Willy, a Paris writer and man-about-town fifteen years older.

Willy ran a workshop of ghostwriters, and he locked his young wife in a room, so the story goes, until she produced the pages. The result was *Claudine at School* (1900), a lively, saucy novel about a schoolgirl, published under his name. It was a huge success, with three sequels, and Claudine collars and perfumes went on sale. Colette got neither the credit nor the money.

She left him, and supported herself as a music-hall dancer and mime, performing in Paris and on tour, and writing under her own name. Her novels made her one of the most loved writers in France. *Chéri* (1920) is the story of a love affair between an aging courtesan and a beautiful, spoiled young man half her age; *The Vagabond* draws on her life on the stage; *Gigi* (1944) is about a girl being raised to be a courtesan who insists on marriage instead. Her memoirs of her mother and childhood are among her finest books.

She married three times, had a daughter, and lived surrounded by cats. In old age, crippled by arthritis, she wrote from a daybed in her apartment overlooking the gardens of the Palais-Royal. When she died in 1954, the Church refused her a religious funeral, because of her divorces and her life; the French state gave her a state funeral instead, the first ever held for a woman in France.
