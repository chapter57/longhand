James Joyce was born in Dublin in 1882, the eldest of ten surviving children of a charming, hard-drinking father who steadily lost the family's money. They moved from house to house, each poorer than the last. Joyce was educated by Jesuits, studied languages at University College Dublin, and left Ireland in 1904 with Nora Barnacle, a young woman from Galway who worked at a hotel. He spent the rest of his life abroad, in Trieste, Zurich and Paris, writing about the city he had left.

*Dubliners* (1914) is a book of fifteen stories about ordinary lives in a city he called the center of paralysis. It took nearly ten years to find a publisher, because printers feared libel and indecency. It ends with "The Dead," a story about a party on a winter's night, and one of the most beautiful endings in English, as snow falls over all of Ireland.

*A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man* (1916) follows Stephen Dedalus, a version of Joyce, from childhood to his decision to leave Ireland and become a writer. Then came *Ulysses* (1922), which follows Leopold Bloom, an advertising salesman, through a single day in Dublin, June 16, 1904, the day of Joyce's first date with Nora. It is built on Homer's *Odyssey*, and it tries nearly every style English allows. It was banned in the United States until 1933.

Joyce suffered from terrible eye disease and had a dozen operations. His last book, *Finnegans Wake* (1939), took seventeen years. He died in Zurich on January 13, 1941, and Dublin now celebrates June 16 every year as Bloomsday.
