Edith Newbold Jones was born in New York into one of the old, wealthy families that ruled the city's society, people who did not work, did not discuss money, and did not approve of women who wrote books. She read her way through her father's library and was writing stories by eleven. She married Teddy Wharton, a sociable Boston banker who shared none of her interests, and the marriage slowly fell apart over nearly thirty years.

Her first book, *The Decoration of Houses* (1897), written with an architect, argued for simplicity and proportion in place of Victorian clutter. She later designed her own house, the Mount, in Lenox, Massachusetts, where she wrote in bed every morning.

*The House of Mirth* (1905), her first great success, follows Lily Bart, a beautiful young woman without money who must marry well and cannot bring herself to, and is destroyed by the society that raised her for nothing else. *Ethan Frome* (1911) is a short, wintry tragedy of a New England farm. *The Age of Innocence* (1920) returns to the New York of the 1870s, where Newland Archer, engaged to a proper young woman, falls in love with her unconventional cousin. It won the Pulitzer Prize in 1921, the first given to a woman for a novel.

She divorced in 1913 and moved to France, where during the First World War she organized relief for refugees and was awarded the Legion of Honor. She wrote more than forty books, and died near Paris in 1937. Her friend Henry James said she wrote with a pen of the finest steel.
