We met Lewis Carroll [on November 26](/almanac/11-26-alices-adventures-under-ground/), the day in 1864 he gave Alice Liddell the handwritten book that became *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*. Today is his birthday, and the rest of his life.

Charles Lutwidge Dodgson was born in the parsonage at Daresbury, in Cheshire, the eldest son among eleven children of a clergyman. He grew up entertaining his brothers and sisters with games, puppet shows and handwritten family magazines full of poems and parodies. He stammered all his life, and was more at ease with children than with adults.

At Oxford he became a lecturer in mathematics at Christ Church, where he lived for nearly fifty years. He wrote books on geometry and algebra, and later on logic, including *Symbolic Logic* and a set of puzzles so ingenious that logicians still study them. He invented word games, ciphers and a game called Doublets, in which you change one word into another a letter at a time.

He was also one of the best portrait photographers of the Victorian age, in the early, difficult days of glass plates and chemicals, and photographed the painters, poets and children of his circle, including Tennyson and Alice herself. *Through the Looking-Glass* (1871) gave us the Jabberwocky, Tweedledum and Tweedledee, Humpty Dumpty and the Red Queen, and *The Hunting of the Snark* (1876) is one of the great nonsense poems. He died in 1898, in Guildford, of pneumonia. He had once said that a book without pictures or conversations was of no use; his have plenty of both.
