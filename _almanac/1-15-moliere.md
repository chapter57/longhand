Jean-Baptiste Poquelin was baptized in Paris on January 15, 1622, the son of an upholsterer to the king. He was expected to take over the family business and was given a good education. At twenty-one he gave it all up to start a theater company with an actress, Madeleine Béjart, and took a stage name: Molière. The company went bankrupt within two years, and he was briefly jailed for its debts.

For thirteen years the troupe toured the provinces of France, and Molière learned his craft as actor, manager and writer of farces. In 1658 they played before the young King Louis XIV, who loved them, and the company settled in Paris under royal protection.

There Molière wrote the comedies that are still performed around the world. *Tartuffe* (1664) is about a religious hypocrite who worms his way into a family, wins the trust of its father, and nearly ruins them. Powerful churchmen were so offended that it was banned for five years. *The Misanthrope* is about a man who hates the flattery and falseness of society so much that he cannot stand anyone, including the woman he loves. *The Miser* is about a man who loves his money more than his children. In all of them, comedy is a way of telling the truth about vanity, greed and pretension.

Molière was ill for years with a lung disease. In February 1673, playing the title role in his own *The Imaginary Invalid*, about a hypochondriac, he collapsed on stage, finished the performance, and died at home a few hours later. Because actors were then denied Christian burial without renouncing their profession, he was buried quietly, at night. French is still sometimes called "the language of Molière."
